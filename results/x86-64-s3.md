# X86-64-S3 — the address and IP widening, landed inert

**Unit:** `HANDOFF=v86c-x86-64-s3`, slice **S3** of `results/x86-64-plan.md` (§4's state
inventory = what is 32-bit-shaped and its verdict per field; §6's S3 row = the specification and
the gate; §5.1 = the 32-bit physical bound the projections below are named after; §7's **R2** =
the risk this slice's instruments exist for).  S0-S2 are landed (`results/x86-64-s0.md`,
`results/x86-64-s1-s2.md`); S4-S13 are untouched.

**The claim, one sentence:** the fetch cursor, the flat-address fields and the segment bases are
64 bits wide, the virtual→physical path takes a 64-bit address and yields a 32-bit one **at one
named projection** (guarded by a named long-mode stop, §3.2), and **nothing 32-bit can observe the
difference** — proved by the whole existing battery being unmoved (transcript `8bb8eca5…`,
determinism 4,000,096,757, bench 200,095,214 with the JIT inlining pins, nasm 118/118) plus the
poison probe extended over the new fields, plus a **new invariant instrument**: "the high half is
zero outside long mode", asserted at **all four mode boundaries** (reset, `CR0`, `CR4`,
`WRMSR(EFER)`) and at both ends of every batch, shown able to fail name-by-name **and reached
through each boundary's own guest-reachable body** (§3.1).

Claims are tagged **MEASURED** (run on this host in this pass, the command or artifact named),
**READ** (from source, `file:line`) or **INTERPRETATION**.  Decisions are marked as decisions.
The shipped binary's sha256 at the end of this slice is `62658fa8…` (MEASURED, `sha256sum
build/v86c`) and **every gate in §4 was run against that binary** (the binary sha was re-checked
after the last source edit; earlier passes on intermediate binaries are not what §4 reports).
**Round 2** (§8) is the review's four findings: three were real defects in this slice's own
claims — a mode-boundary instrument that did not exist, a projection with no long-mode guard and a
premise sentence wider than its evidence — and one was an undeclared behaviour change the review
caught in the decode window.  All four are fixed and each has an arm that fails without its fix.

## 2. What landed, field by field (plan §4's verdicts, discharged)

- **`instruction_pointer`, `previous_ip` → `uint64_t`.** `previous_ip` needed no masking: every
  store into it is either the (already projected) `instruction_pointer` or a uop's 32-bit `eip`
  (`rg 'previous_ip\s*=' src/cpu`, READ: 14 sites, all of that shape).
- **`last_virt_eip`, `eip_phys` → `uint64_t`.** The fetch trick generalises unchanged: `eip_phys =
  translate(eip) ^ eip` cancels the page bits, and `phys = (uint32_t)(eip_phys ^ eip)` is the
  projection at the `mem8` boundary (`cpu.c:96-115`).  The sentinel moved from `-1` to
  `VIRT_EIP_NONE` (`0x00000000FFFFFFFF`): `-1` in a 64-bit field is `0xFFFFFFFFFFFFFFFF`, which is
  itself a non-zero high half — the leak the invariant exists to catch, written by the reset path.
  Its low 12 bits are set, which no page value can be, so it cannot collide; every reader compares
  at page granularity or through `(uint32_t)`, so each verdict is unchanged (MEASURED: the
  transcript is byte-identical).  **This re-pins three `cpu_selftest` checks**
  (`0xffffffffffffffff → 0xffffffff`) — the one existing pin this slice moved, and it is a
  *sentinel spelling*, not an observable (§5.1).
- **`segment_offsets[8]` → `uint64_t`.** `segment_limits` stays 32-bit (plan §4).  `get_seg_cs`/
  `get_seg_ss` return `uint64_t` because their callers ADD an offset and store the sum in a widened
  field; `get_seg`/`get_seg_prefix*` stay `int32_t` because they feed the 32-bit ADDRESSING FORMS,
  whose arithmetic is upstream's i32 and wraps (decision, `modrm.h`).  `seg_descriptor_base` became
  `uint32_t`: signed, a base of `0xFFF00000` would sign-extend to `0xFFFFFFFFFFF00000` at the store.
- **`idtr_offset`, `gdtr_offset` → `uint64_t`.** `lgdt`/`lidt` store `(uint32_t)offset & (uint32_t)mask`;
  `sgdt`/`sidt` write `(int32_t)((uint32_t)base & (uint32_t)mask)` — upstream's i32 arithmetic made
  explicit.  The 64-bit 10-byte `LGDT` operand is S11's.
- **`translate_address*` signatures.** Parameters `uint64_t`, result still `uint32_t`.  The
  asymmetry IS the model (plan §5.1) and the projection happens **once**, inside `translate_address`
  (`paging.c:341-370`), with a comment naming it: outside long mode a linear address is 32 bits, and
  the low 32 bits of a 64-bit sum are the sum mod 2³² — i.e. exactly upstream's i32 wrap, for every
  value a 32-bit guest can form.  A 4-level PTE naming a physical address above 4 GiB stays S11's
  named refusal.
- **The uop block key.** `uop_block_t.virt_start`/`stop_eip`, `uop_t.eip`/`body_ip`,
  `uop_cblock_t.virt_start` and the decoder's window/cursor are 64-bit; `uop_hash_pos` folds BOTH
  halves (a hash taking only the low half would keep two blocks apart in the compare but identical
  in the slot — a silent never-hits cliff, not a wrong answer).  The hash keeps its old constants
  for the low half, so every 32-bit key hashes to the same slot as before (MEASURED: the bench's
  `uop` statistics and the 7,934,349-check cache selftest are unmoved).
- **The JIT's `eip` stores.** `jitcg_store_u64` writes the whole 8-byte field: `mov r32, imm32`
  zero-extends into the host register, then an 8-byte store.  `mov r/m64, imm32` would sign-extend,
  which is WRONG for this value class (`0xFFFFFFF0` must become `0x00000000FFFFFFF0`).  Stated, not
  implied: the compiled code's **reads** of `instruction_pointer` (page test, iteration limit,
  divergence compare, the chain's ip compare) stay 32-bit and the JIT's own key stays 32-bit —
  exact while the high half is zero, which is the invariant, and the widening of the JIT is S13's
  (named at the boundary, `uop_cache.c`'s `jit_compile_block` call site).

## 3. The new instrument: "the high half is zero outside long mode"

Plan §6's S3 row asks for it and §7's R2 is why.  Design:

- `cpu_address_widening_leak()` returns the **name** of the field whose high half is non-zero
  (six scalars, and each of the eight segment slots by name, since an off-by-one stride lands on a
  neighbour — see §5.2), or NULL.  It answers NULL unconditionally when `EFER.LMA` is set: in
  IA-32e the high half is where the address lives.  **Nothing can set LMA before S5**, so that arm
  is unreachable in the shipped tree and is exercised by putting the state there (stated, not
  implied — the S2 discipline).
- `cpu_assert_address_widening(where)` turns a leak into the machine's **named stop**
  (`unimplemented_instruction`), so a widening mistake is a stop with a field name instead of a
  wrong address in one guest.
- It is called at the **four mode boundaries** — `reset_cpu`, `set_cr0` (after the commit),
  `instr_0F22`'s `CR4` write (after the commit), `instr_0F30`'s `EFER` write (after the commit) —
  **and at both ends of every `cycle_internal` batch**.  The batch calls are what make it a
  continuous run-time instrument; the *end* call is the load-bearing one, and §5.3 shows why (a
  compiled block's leak can be repaired by a later uop's own store before the batch ends — the
  entry call alone misses it).
  **ROUND 2: THIS WAS A CLAIM WITHOUT A SEAT.**  The first version of this report and of
  `cpu_state.h` both said the three transitions were covered; `rg cpu_assert_address_widening`
  found only `reset_cpu` and the two batch sites (review S1).  The sites are now added, and —
  because a seat that is never called is indistinguishable from an absent seat — `cpu_selftest`'s
  arm (7) drives **each of the three bodies a guest reaches** (`instr_0F30`, `set_cr0`,
  `instr_0F22`) with `gdtr_offset`'s high half poisoned and requires the stop to name both the
  field and the boundary; with `set_cr0`'s call deleted the arm fails 3 checks (MEASURED).
- `set_instruction_pointer()` (`cpu_state.h`) is the only producer of a new eip: it projects to 32
  bits outside long mode and does not in long mode.  **This is what keeps the invariant from being a
  tautology**: the one way left to get a non-zero high half is a store that did not come through
  the function, which is exactly the class a future slice adds.
- **Able to fail:** `cpu_selftest`'s `test_address_widening_invariant` seeds ONE field's high half
  into a reset machine, 14 times, and requires its own name back; asserts the clean machine answers
  NULL; asserts the long-mode arm accepts the same poison; and asserts the armed form reaches the
  stop channel while the disarmed form (`--poison`) does not.  `cpu_selftest` 3846 → **3873** checks
  (MEASURED).
- **Its stated limit:** it reads the *state at the batch boundary*, so a leak that survives in no
  field at any boundary is invisible to it — the whole-state differential and the poison probe are
  the instruments there.  It also cannot see a leak in a *died* field's neighbour the way a
  byte-level stride check would; that class is caught by the battery (§5.2).

### 3.2 The projection's guard, which the invariant is not (round 2, N4)

The invariant answers NULL **when `EFER.LMA` is set**, by design.  So it does **not** guard
`translate_address`'s `(uint32_t)` projection in long mode — and `paging.h` said it did.  That was
the second wrong claim: in the one mode where the projection would be wrong (a 64-bit linear
address), nothing was checking it.  Fixed by adding the guard where it belongs: `translate_address`
and its write-path twin (`translate_address_write_and_can_skip_dirty`) now **stop by name** —
`"translate_address: long mode needs the 4-level walk (plan ss6 S11)"` — and set the abort flag
(`get_seg`'s precedent: the flag is the port's abort channel even when the cause is not a page
fault, so the instruction does not act on the `0` returned), until S11 lands the real walk.
Unreachable today and *shown* so rather than assumed: S2 refuses the three writes that could enter
IA-32e and nothing sets LMA.

### 3.1 The poison probe, extended

`--poison` now also seeds the high half of `instruction_pointer`, `previous_ip`, `last_virt_eip`,
`eip_phys`, `idtr_offset`, `gdtr_offset` and all eight `segment_offsets[]` slots (`| 0xA5A5A5A5 << 32`
— the LOW half keeps the boot's value, or the first data access would move).  It disarms the
invariant while it runs: the two instruments point in opposite directions and are each other's
control — the probe proves *a present high half changes nothing*, the invariant proves *the shipped
paths never produce one*.  MEASURED: the M0 transcript is byte-identical with and without the seed,
`8bb8eca53f5c5ccb == 8bb8eca53f5c5ccb`.

**What it still cannot reach, stated so its silence is not read as a proof:** the M0 image is flat
with paging off, so `segment_offsets[FS]/[GS]/[TR]/[LDTR]`, `idtr_offset` and `gdtr_offset` are
seeded but barely read, and no page walk runs to consult the TLB fields; R8-R15 do not exist yet
(S5); and the flag operands' seed is inert by construction (S1's note, unchanged).

## 5. What the battery caught — the three findings of this slice

Every one of these was a *silent* defect the widening introduced, and every one was caught by an
existing instrument.  They are the reason this slice's gate is the whole battery and not a new test.

### 5.1 The three re-pinned checks (deliberate, named)

`cpu_selftest`'s three `last_virt_eip == -1` checks moved to `== VIRT_EIP_NONE`
(`0xffffffffffffffff → 0x00000000ffffffff`).  Not a leak: the *sentinel's spelling* changed because
`-1` in a 64-bit field is a high-half leak by construction.  Every reader is unchanged (§2), and the
behavioural proof is the byte-identical transcript.  Precedent: S2 moved one `cpu_m1c_selftest`
check for the same reason and said so.

### 5.2 A stride constant on a widened array — `jit_codegen.c`, caught by `jit_codegen_selftest`

`jitcg_emit_addr` added a segment base with `JC_OFF(segment_offsets) + 4 * seg`.  With `uint64_t[8]`
that lands inside the array for `seg` 0-3, so the compiled path silently added the **wrong
segment's** base.  MEASURED red: the prefix pin (`mov eax, fs:[0x1400]`, FS base `0x40000`, DS base
`0`) failed in the compiled arm only, as `reg64[0] 0xa5a5a5a5 vs 0x12345678` and
`ram32(TRACE) 0x12345678` — the DS word instead of the FS one.  Fixed to `8 * seg`; the ADD stays
32-bit (exact while the base's high half is zero; the 64-bit addressing form is S7's).
**This is the class a byte-level audit does not see, and it is why the plan's S3 gate is the
battery.**  The interpreter and uop-cache arms were green throughout: the defect existed in one
engine only.

### 5.3 A width change nobody wrote — `jit_codegen.c`, caught by the same suite

`rec_jcc`/`rec_jmp` stored `u->eip + (uint32_t)u->len + (uint32_t)u->imm`.  Every operand was
`uint32_t`, so a rel8 `-2` was the two's-complement value it looks like and the sum wrapped at 32
bits.  Widening `uop_t.eip` to `uint64_t` turned the same expression into `+ 4294967294`: the chain
test wrote `instruction_pointer = 0x1_0000_1000`, and the whole-state compare went red at **offset
204** — `last_virt_eip`'s high half.  Fixed by `jitcg_target32` (sign-extend the displacement, wrap
to the JIT's 32-bit ip), with the arithmetic's width now spelled in one place.

**And this is the falsifier that shows the new invariant is live:** with the broken expression
restored, the batch-**entry** call sees nothing (a later uop's own ip store repairs the field inside
the same batch) while the batch-**end** call stops the machine by name — MEASURED, reported as
`widened address field eip_phys has a non-zero high half outside long mode (at cycle_internal (batch
end))`.  That is why the end-of-batch call exists.

### 5.4 A field missing from the reset state — `reset_cpu`, caught by the new invariant

`reset_cpu` never cleared `eip_phys` (upstream does not either: it is dead until `last_virt_eip`
matches an eip, and the reset writes the sentinel).  With the widening the field can now carry a
high half from a *previous machine in the same process*, and the invariant named it at
`reset_cpu` on the selftest's second reset.  Reset now zeroes it — unobservable (the pair is
invalidated by the sentinel) and it makes the reset state canonically clean, which is the premise
the mode-boundary assertion needs.

## 6. ROUND 2 — the review's findings, one arm each

The review found **no blocker and two should-fix**, and I agree with all of them: three of the
four are this slice's *own claims* being wider than its code, which is the failure mode the
house rules name first.  Each fix has an arm that fails without it.

### 6.1 S1 — the mode-boundary instrument did not exist (fixed by ADDING the seats)

`rg cpu_assert_address_widening` found three call sites (reset, batch entry, batch end) while
`cpu_state.h` and this report both claimed four boundaries.  A comment describing a seat nobody
calls is the exact defect this tree keeps finding, so the claim is not reworded: **the three sites
are added** (`paging.c` `set_cr0` after the commit, `instructions_0f.c`'s `CR4` write and
`WRMSR(EFER)` write after theirs), and the selftest's arm (7) drives each boundary's own
guest-reachable body and requires the stop to name the field **and** the boundary.
**Both arms, MEASURED:** with the sites present — `cpu_selftest` 3885/0; with `set_cr0`'s call
deleted — 3 failures (`unimplemented_calls == 1 (got 0x0)` and both name checks), i.e. the seat is
load-bearing and its absence is visible.

### 6.2 S2 — an undeclared behaviour change in the decode window (declared, tested)

`uop_cache.c`'s window end was `(virt_start | 0xFFF) + 1` in a `uint32_t`, which **wraps to 0 for
`virt_start` in 0xFFFFF000..0xFFFFFFFF**: `dec_fetch` failed on the first byte, `n == 0`,
`uop_block_decode()` returned NULL and the interpreter ran — every time.  S3's masked form gives a
real window, so a block at the last page now **decodes (and compiles)**.  That is a fix, not a
regression, but it changes **which engine runs a legal guest** — reachable by any 32-bit guest that
pages the last page into RAM — so it is now declared in three places: the code comment at the site
(`uop_cache.c`, with what it could change), this section, and two tests:

- `uop_cache_selftest`'s `last_page_tests`: a block at the top page decodes with the **full 64-bit
  address** in its uops (`eip`, `body_ip`, `stop_eip` = 0xFFFFFxxx / 2^32 — a value the old 32-bit
  field could not hold), **the window still ends at the page** (an instruction needing the byte
  past 0xFFFFFFFF is dropped, `n == 0`, `PAGE_END`), and the minimum case (a 1-byte `hlt` at
  0xFFFFF000) decodes.
- `jit_codegen_selftest`'s `test_last_page`: the **three arms** run `mov eax,0x1234; hlt` from the
  last page through a 4 KiB page table, agree on the **whole cpu state and guest RAM**, and the
  counters show **which engine ran**: `decodes interp=0 uop=1 compiled=1`, `blocks compiled 1`,
  `eip 0xfffff006` in all three.
- **Both arms, MEASURED:** with the pre-fix expression restored, the unit test fails 8 checks and
  the three-arm test fails 4 (`arm_decodes[1] >= 1`, `arm_decodes[2] >= 1`, `blocks_compiled >= 1`,
  `uop_last_stop == BLOCK_END` got `PAGE_END`) with `decodes interp=0 uop=0 compiled=0` — **while
  the three arms still agreed on the state**, which is exactly the claim: engine *selection*
  changed, semantics did not.

### 6.3 N3 — the detector's premise was wider than its evidence (the sentence fixed)

`cpu_state.h` claimed `set_instruction_pointer` was the only producer and backed it with an `rg`
that misses ten `instruction_pointer = previous_ip` copies and the JIT's five *emitted* stores.
The detector is still total; the sentence was wrong.  It now **enumerates the three classes** —
(1) computed through the function, (2) copies of an already-projected value, (3) the JIT's
compile-time 32-bit values stored eight bytes wide — and says which of them could introduce a new
high half (only a store that is none of the three).

### 6.4 N4 — the projection had no long-mode guard (fixed with the named stop)

§3.2.  `paging.h`'s signature note said the mode-boundary invariant guarded the cast; it does not
(it answers NULL under LMA).  Both `translate_address` and its write-path twin now stop by name.

### 6.5 N5 — a poisoned state left behind by the selftest (fixed)

`test_address_widening_invariant` left `gdtr_offset`'s high half poisoned; `reset_cpu` asserts the
invariant, so a test inserted after it would aborted the binary.  The test now ends with
`cpu_state_init()`.

### 6.6 N6/N7 — recorded, not fixed (see §7)

The `--poison` disarm of the invariant, and the ~30 remaining implicit `uint64_t → int32_t`
narrowings in the 32-bit addressing forms.  Both are in §7's residual list with their reasons.

## 7. What did NOT land, stated so its absence is not a promise

1. **The JIT is 32-bit-eip throughout.**  Its stores are 8-byte (§2) but its ip *comparisons*, its
   own block key, `jit_compile_block`'s signature and the recipes' `mov r32, imm32` ip values are
   32-bit.  Exact while the high half is zero — the invariant — and S13's, named at each boundary.
2. **The decoder's addressing forms are 32-bit.**  `resolve_modrm16/32`, `get_seg_prefix_ds/_ss`,
   `uop_materialize_addr`, `safe_read*`/`safe_write*` parameters and the stack helpers all still
   compute in 32-bit linear addresses, projected at their `int32_t` boundary (upstream's wrap,
   commented at each of the two accessor families).  The 64-bit address generation is S5/S7's.
3. **The page walk is still 32-bit**: `do_page_walk(int32_t)` — plan §4 gives it "WIDEN + one level"
   in S11, and the 1M-entry `tlb_data` stays as it is (§4's PARALLEL verdict).
4. **`prefixes` is still `uint8_t`** (REX needs 4 more bits; §4 puts that with S5's decode).
5. **`cr[2]/cr[3]` are still `int32_t`** — the physical model's bound (§5.1), a decision §4 records;
   `trigger_pagefault` projects the faulting linear address into CR2 with a comment naming S11's
   64-bit CR2 as the gap.
6. **`EFER.LMA`'s transition, `CPU_MODE_COMPAT`, the FS/GS base MSRs, `SYSCALL`/`SYSRET`**: S11's.
   The widened `segment_offsets` slots are the seat they will land in.
7. **No new pinned value was created and none was weakened**: the poison probe's assertion, the
   transcript's hash, the determinism and bench counts and the JIT inlining pins are the values S0-S2
   left.  (One *sentinel spelling* moved — §5.1 — and it is named there.)
8. **THE DECODE WINDOW'S TOP-PAGE BEHAVIOUR CHANGE IS REAL, not inert** (§6.2): a guest that pages
   the last page into RAM now gets the cached/compiled engine where it always got the interpreter.
   Declared, tested in both directions, and named here because "inert slice" is the plan's word for
   S3 and this is the exception to it.
9. **N6, THE `--poison` DISARM, recorded not fixed.**  `--poison` sets
   `cpu_address_widening_check_enabled = false`, so the diagnostic flag turns the invariant off.  It
   has to (the probe seeds on purpose what the invariant forbids, and the two are each other's
   control), but the consequence is worth stating: **a run under `--poison` is not
   invariant-checked**, so `--poison` cannot also be evidence about leaks.  The gates keep them
   apart — `make poison-probe` runs both binaries and compares; every other gate runs armed.
10. **N7, THE ~30 IMPLICIT `uint64_t → int32_t` NARROWINGS, recorded not fixed.**  The 32-bit
   addressing forms and the stack helpers project at their `int32_t` boundary (item 2 above), and a
   `-Wconversion -Wsign-conversion` sweep enumerates them.  They are exact mod 2³² — upstream's own
   i32 wrap — but they are *implicit*: a future slice that widens the addressing form must find them
   by sweep, not by the compiler's default diagnostics.  Two rules cover them today, and they are
   stated at the two accessor families (`modrm.h`, `misc_instr.c`'s stack helpers).
